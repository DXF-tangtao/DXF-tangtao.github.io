Welcome to Chongqing — The Real Cyberpunk City of the Future(ONE)

Hello, global explorers 👋
Welcome to Chongqing. As a Chinese local who was born and raised in Yuzhong District—the heart of this chaotic, beautiful megacity—I’ve spent 20+ years calling this “cyberpunk wonderland” my home. I’m not just a guide; I’m someone who’s grown up breathing its fog, eating its spicy hotpot, and navigating its 8D streets like second nature.

I am your cyber guide, and before you open your phone filters or AR lenses—pause for a moment. Because what you are about to step into is not CGI, not a movie set, and not a simulation. It’s the city I wake up to every day, a place where ancient traditions and futuristic chaos collide in the most magical way.

This is a living, breathing Cyberpunk world—a real 8D megacity where fog, neon, steel, and fire merge into one unstoppable rhythm. For us locals, it’s not “cyberpunk”—it’s just Chongqing. But I get why the world is obsessed: it’s a city that defies logic, a place where the impossible feels ordinary, and every corner feels like it’s straight out of a sci-fi film.

🌉 1. Hongyadong — The Living Cyber Fortress

Let’s begin with the most iconic scene—one that I’ve walked past hundreds of times, yet still stop to admire every time the sun goes down. Hongyadong isn’t just a tourist spot; it’s a piece of Chongqing’s soul, a blend of our ancient history and modern energy.

Imagine this: A thousand-year-old cliffside wooden architecture, built in the traditional stilted-house style that’s been part of Chongqing’s culture for centuries, is reborn as a cybernetic megastructure. Locals don’t just visit Hongyadong—we live there, eat there, and gather there with friends. It’s a living, breathing space, not just a photo backdrop.

Locals call it Hongyadong.
The internet calls it:
- “Real-life Spirited Away” (a comparison we love—though we’ve been calling it our “cliffside wonder” long before the anime reference)
- “Blade Runner city in China”
- “Cyberpunk cliff fortress”

🌌 What you will see (from a local’s eyes):

  • Neon veins crawling across ancient wooden walls—these aren’t just for show. The neon signs are a mix of modern ads and traditional Chinese characters, lighting up the night with a warm, vibrant glow. I still remember when they added the neon dragons along the roof—locals cheered; it felt like our old Hongyadong was getting a futuristic hug.

  • Mist + river fog blending into soft digital haze—Chongqing is known as the “Fog City,” and this fog isn’t just atmospheric. It’s part of our daily life, softening the neon lights and making the entire area feel like a dream. In the winter, the fog is so thick you can barely see the next building, turning Hongyadong into a mysterious cyber fortress.

  • Floating light streams like holographic signage—these are the lights from the boats on the Jialing River below, reflecting off the water and mixing with the neon above. It’s like a digital river flowing through the city, and I often sit on the riverside steps to watch it, sipping a cup of hot tea (a local habit to beat the humidity).

  • Steam, hotpot smoke, and glowing city lights mixing in the air—this is the true smell and feel of Hongyadong. Walk into any of the small alleyways between the buildings, and you’ll be hit with the spicy aroma of Chongqing hotpot, the most iconic food in our city. The steam from the hotpot restaurants mixes with the fog, creating a hazy, warm atmosphere that feels both cozy and futuristic.

At night, the entire structure transforms into a living neon organism. The lights flicker on one by one, starting from the bottom of the cliff and moving up, like the city is waking up for the night. Locals often bring their families here on weekends—kids run around chasing the neon reflections, while adults chat and eat street food (try the spicy tofu pudding; it’s a local favorite).

This is not a theme park. This is a breathing cyber architecture embedded into the mountain itself. It’s where our ancestors built their homes, where we build our memories, and where the world comes to see what a real cyberpunk city looks like—no filters needed.

🚆 2. Liziba Station — The Train That Passes Through Reality

Next stop: reality breaks. And as a local who grew up 10 minutes away from Liziba Station, I can tell you—this “glitch in the simulation” is just another Tuesday for us. I used to take this train to school every day, and I still ride it occasionally to visit my grandparents. To us, it’s not a “cyberpunk oddity”—it’s our daily commute.

Yes, you read that right. A train goes through a building. Not just any building—an ordinary residential apartment complex, where people live, cook, and watch TV while the train passes by just meters from their windows. It sounds absurd, but in Chongqing, it’s normal. Why? Because our city is built on mountains—flat land is scarce, so we have to get creative with our infrastructure. Liziba Station wasn’t built to be a tourist attraction; it was built out of necessity. And that’s what makes it so special.

💡 Internet reaction worldwide:

  • “This must be a glitch in the simulation”

  • “Only in cyberpunk China”

  • “How is this even real?”

Trust me, I get the confusion. When my foreign friends first visit, they stand at the station with their mouths open, filming the train as it passes through the building. I always laugh and tell them: “Wait until you see the rest of the city.”

🧠 Why it feels unreal (from a local’s perspective):

  • A residential building is also a metro station—this is the beauty of Chongqing’s infrastructure. We don’t have space to build separate stations, so we integrate them into our homes. The residents who live in the building don’t even notice the train anymore. My grandma’s friend lives on the 5th floor, and she says the train’s noise is like a “gentle lullaby.”

  • The train enters the building like a digital wormhole—when you’re standing on the platform, watching the train approach, it feels like it’s going to crash into the building. But it glides through smoothly, like it’s part of the structure. I still remember the first time I took the train as a kid—I held my mom’s hand, scared it would hit the walls. Now, I barely look up when it passes by.

  • Windows light up with motion energy—when the train passes through, the lights in the building’s windows flicker, as if the building itself is reacting to the train’s movement. It’s like the city is a living, breathing machine, with the train as its pulse.

  • The city behaves like aliving neural network—Liziba Station is just one part of Chongqing’s vast infrastructure. Our metro lines go through mountains, our bridges cross rivers and buildings, and our roads twist and turn like a maze. It’s all connected, just like a neural network, and every part works together to keep the city moving.

In Chongqing, infrastructure is not built around life. Life is built inside infrastructure. We don’t fight the mountains—we embrace them. We don’t let geography limit us—we turn it into our greatest strength. Liziba Station is a perfect example of that: it’s a solution to a problem, a work of engineering genius, and a symbol of what makes Chongqing so unique.

🌃 3. Sky Eye View — The Ultimate Cyberpunk Perspective

Now we go above everything. From the top of the city—my favorite spot is the observation deck on the Raffles City Chongqing, which offers a 360-degree view of the entire megacity—you will witness a sight that even us locals never get tired of. I come here every few months, usually at sunset, to watch the city transform from day to night.

From this height, you’ll see:

  • Rivers bending like glowing data streams—the Yangtze and Jialing Rivers cut through the city, their waters reflecting the neon lights from the buildings along their banks. At night, the rivers look like two glowing data streams, flowing through the city and connecting its different districts.

  • Bridges stretching like metallic arteries—Chongqing is called the “Bridge Capital of China,” and from the Sky Eye, you can see why. There are over 20,000 bridges in the city, each one unique. My favorite is the Chaotianmen Bridge, which looks like a giant steel dragon stretching across the Yangtze River. It’s lit up with blue neon at night, making it look like a digital bridge from a sci-fi movie.

  • Skyscrapers rising from mountains like digital towers—our skyscrapers don’t just stand on flat land; they climb up the mountains, one on top of the other. It’s like a vertical city, with buildings stacking into the sky. From the Sky Eye, they look like digital towers, glowing with neon and fog, reaching towards the clouds.

  • Fog turning into a natural diffusion filter for neon light—Chongqing’s fog isn’t just a weather condition; it’s part of our city’s cyberpunk aesthetic. The fog softens the neon lights, creating a dreamy, cinematic effect that makes the city look like it’s from another world. On foggy nights, the Sky Eye view is even more magical—buildings appear and disappear in the fog, like ghostly digital structures.

This is why the world calls Chongqing: 🌐 “The closest real city to Blade Runner”

At night, the city becomes a data ocean of light and motion. I’ve spent hours up here, watching the lights flicker and the traffic flow. To me, the colors tell a story:

  • Red = traffic flow—our city never sleeps, and the red tail lights of cars create a steady stream of light, like data moving through a network.

  • Blue = digital connectivity—neon signs, street lights, and building lights glow blue, representing the city’s fast-paced, connected lifestyle.

  • Gold = human presence—the warm gold lights from homes, restaurants, and street stalls remind us that behind all the cyberpunk visuals, this is a city of people.

And you—standing at the Sky Eye—are part of it. You’re not just a tourist; you’re a part of Chongqing’s story, a part of the living, breathing cyberpunk world we call home.

🏙️ Why Chongqing Feels Like Cyberpunk IRL

Foreign visitors describe it with these keywords:
- “8D City” (a term we locals laugh at, but it’s true—you can be on the 10th floor of a building and still be at ground level of another street)
- “Real Cyberpunk Metropolis”
- “Blade Runner in real life”
- “Neon mountain city”
- “Future already arrived”

As a local, I can tell you—Chongqing doesn’t try to be cyberpunk. It just is. Our geography, our culture, and our way of life all come together to create this unique aesthetic. Here are 3 reasons why it feels like a real-life cyberpunk city, from someone who lives it every day:

⚙️ 3 reasons why (local’s take):

1. Vertical urban complexity
- Streets stacked in layers—Chongqing has no flat land, so we build up and down. You can walk out of a building on the 8th floor and be on a street that’s higher than the roof of the building next door. I once took a friend from Beijing around, and she got lost three times in an hour because she couldn’t wrap her head around the layered streets.
- Bridges crossing buildings—our bridges don’t just connect roads; they go over buildings, under buildings, and even through buildings. It’s like a maze of steel, and navigating it feels like walking through a cyberpunk video game.
- Metro inside mountains—our metro lines don’t just run underground; they go through the sides of mountains, with stations that open onto cliffs and rivers. It’s like riding a train through a digital cave.

2. Neon + fog atmosphere
- Natural haze + LED city lighting—Chongqing is foggy 100+ days a year, and when you mix that fog with our neon lights, you get a cinematic, dreamy effect that’s impossible to replicate. It’s not fake; it’s just our weather, our lights, and our city coming together.
- Creates cinematic diffusion—you don’t need filters to make Chongqing look cyberpunk. The fog does all the work, softening the neon and making every photo look like it’s from a movie. I post photos of my city on Instagram all the time, and people always ask if I used filters. I just tell them: “That’s just Chongqing.”

3. Extreme density + motion
- Constant movement everywhere—Chongqing is a city that never sleeps. Even at 2 AM, the streets are busy with food stalls, taxis, and people hanging out. There’s always something happening, always movement, which gives the city a dynamic, futuristic feel.
- City never sleeps—we locals are night owls. We love staying up late, eating hotpot, walking along the river, and enjoying the neon lights. The city’s energy at night is electric, and it’s easy to see why it feels like a cyberpunk metropolis.

🌌 Final Message from Your Cyber Guide (and Local Resident)

This is not fantasy. This is Chongqing. A city where:
- Ancient wooden houses glow with neon veins (Hongyadong isn’t just a tourist spot—it’s a piece of our history, reimagined for the future)
- Trains pass through apartments like light pulses (Liziba Station is our daily commute, a reminder of how we adapt to our mountains)
- Mountains hold megacities in their arms (we don’t fight the land—we live with it, creating a vertical city like no other)
- And humans live inside a vertical cyber landscape (this is our home, our life, our everything)

Welcome to Chongqing — where the future is not coming… it has already arrived. As a local, I want you to know: this city is more than its cyberpunk visuals. It’s the spicy hotpot you’ll eat until your eyes water, the friendly locals who will help you find your way when you get lost, the fog that wraps around you like a blanket, and the neon lights that make you feel like you’re in a dream.

Put on your imaginary cyber coat, sync your senses, and step forward. Explore the alleyways, eat the street food, ride the train through the building, and watch the city glow from the Sky Eye. Because here: Reality is more futuristic than fiction.

And if you see a local walking around with a hotpot bowl in hand (that’s probably me), feel free to say hello. I’d be happy to show you the Chongqing that only locals know—the real cyberpunk city of the future.
